Comparing Quantities

Chapter 8 · Grade 8 Mathematics

We compare quantities every day: comparing prices, calculating discounts, figuring out profit or loss, and planning savings with interest. This chapter builds on percentages and ratios from earlier grades to introduce profit and loss, discount, and most importantly simple and compound interest, which are essential for financial literacy.

Ratios and Percentages Review

Ratios

A ratio a : b compares two quantities of the same kind.

a : b = a / b

Ratios can be simplified by dividing by HCF. Example: 24 : 36 = 2 : 3.

Percentages

A percentage is a ratio with denominator 100. "Per cent" means "per hundred".

Percentage = (Part / Whole) × 100%
  • Fraction to %: 3/4 = 75%
  • Decimal to %: 0.65 = 65%
  • % to fraction: 35% = 7/20

Percentage Increase and Decrease

% Increase = (Increase / Original) × 100%
% Decrease = (Decrease / Original) × 100%

Examples:

  • Price: Rs 200 to Rs 250 — Increase = 50; % increase = 25%
  • Price: Rs 500 to Rs 425 — Decrease = 75; % decrease = 15%

Finding Value After Percentage Change

New value = Original × (1 + R/100)   [increase]
New value = Original × (1 - R/100)   [decrease]

Profit, Loss, and Discount

Profit and Loss

CP = Cost Price (price to buy); SP = Selling Price (price when sold).

  • If SP > CP: Profit (gain)
  • If CP > SP: Loss
  • If SP = CP: No profit, no loss
Profit = SP - CP    (when SP > CP)
Loss = CP - SP    (when CP > SP)
Profit% = (Profit / CP) × 100
Loss% = (Loss / CP) × 100

Profit% and Loss% are always calculated on Cost Price.

Example: CP = Rs 800, SP = Rs 1000.
Profit = 200; Profit% = 25%

Discount and Marked Price

Marked Price (MP) is the price printed on the item. Discount is a reduction given on the marked price.

Discount = MP - SP
Discount% = (Discount / MP) × 100
SP = MP - Discount = MP × (1 - Discount%/100)

Example: MP = Rs 500, Discount% = 10%.

Discount = 10% of 500 = Rs 50;   SP = Rs 450

Finding CP from SP

CP = (100 × SP) / (100 + Profit%)
CP = (100 × SP) / (100 - Loss%)

Simple Interest

When money is borrowed or invested, interest is the extra amount paid or earned. In Simple Interest (SI), the interest is calculated only on the original principal for every time period.

SI = (P × R × T) / 100

Where: P = Principal, R = Rate% per annum, T = Time in years.

Amount (A) = P + SI

Worked Example

P = Rs 5000, R = 8% per annum, T = 3 years. Find SI and Amount.

SI = (5000 × 8 × 3) / 100 = Rs 1200
A = 5000 + 1200 = Rs 6200

Finding Unknown Quantities

P = (SI × 100) / (R × T)
R = (SI × 100) / (P × T)
T = (SI × 100) / (P × R)

Example: SI = Rs 600, P = Rs 3000, T = 2 years. Find R.

R = (600 × 100) / (3000 × 2) = 10% per annum

Compound Interest

In Compound Interest (CI), interest is added to the principal at the end of each period, and the next period's interest is calculated on the new, larger principal. Interest earns interest — this is the compounding effect.

Amount: A = P × (1 + R/100)n
CI = A - P = P × [(1 + R/100)n - 1]

Where P = Principal, R = Rate% per annum, n = number of years (compounded annually).

Worked Example

P = Rs 10000, R = 10%, n = 2 years.

A = 10000 × (1.1)² = 10000 × 1.21 = Rs 12100
CI = 12100 - 10000 = Rs 2100

Compare: SI = (10000×10×2)/100 = Rs 2000

CI (Rs 2100) > SI (Rs 2000)

CI vs SI Comparison

  • Year 1: CI = SI (same amount of interest)
  • Year 2 onwards: CI > SI (interest on interest)
  • The difference grows with time
CI - SI = P × (R/100)²    [for 2 years]

Example: P=10000, R=10%, n=2:

CI - SI = 10000 × (0.1)² = Rs 100 ✓

The longer the period, the greater the advantage of CI over SI.

Applications of Compound Interest

The CI formula applies whenever growth or decay happens as a percentage of the current value.

Population Growth

If a population P grows at rate R% per year, after n years:

Pn = P0 × (1 + R/100)n

Example: A town has population 50000. It grows at 4% per year. Find the population after 2 years.

P2 = 50000 × (1.04)²
       = 50000 × 1.0816
       = 54080

The increase in population = 54080 - 50000 = 4080 people.

Depreciation (Value Decrease)

When the value of an asset decreases at R% per year:

Vn = V0 × (1 - R/100)n

Example: A machine costing Rs 80000 depreciates at 10% per year. Find its value after 3 years.

V3 = 80000 × (0.9)³
       = 80000 × 0.729
       = Rs 58320

Mixed Growth and Decay

If rates differ for different periods:

Final = P × (1+R1/100)n1 × (1-R2/100)n2

Summary: Comparing Quantities – All Formulas

Percentages

% = (Part / Whole) × 100
% Increase = (Increase / Original) × 100

Profit and Loss

Profit = SP - CP    Loss = CP - SP
Profit% = (Profit / CP) × 100
Loss% = (Loss / CP) × 100

Discount

Discount = MP - SP
Discount% = (Discount / MP) × 100
SP = MP × (1 - Discount%/100)

Finding CP

CP = 100 × SP / (100 + Profit%)
CP = 100 × SP / (100 - Loss%)

Simple Interest

SI = (P × R × T) / 100
A = P + SI

Compound Interest

A = P × (1 + R/100)n
CI = A - P
CI - SI = P(R/100)²    [for 2 years]

Applications

Population growth: Pn = P0(1 + R/100)n
Depreciation: Vn = V0(1 - R/100)n

Key Notes

  • Profit% and Loss% are on CP; Discount% is on MP
  • CI ≥ SI for same P, R, T; equal only in year 1
  • For CI compounded half-yearly: replace R with R/2 and n with 2n
