Understanding Quadrilaterals

Chapter 3 · Grade 8 Mathematics

Quadrilaterals are four-sided polygons that appear everywhere in the world around us — from tiles and screens to windows and fields. In this chapter we classify polygons, explore their angle properties, and study the special members of the quadrilateral family in detail.

Polygons: Classification and Types

A polygon is a closed plane figure bounded by straight line segments. Polygons are classified by the number of sides:

Classification by Number of Sides

  • 3 sides: Triangle
  • 4 sides: Quadrilateral
  • 5 sides: Pentagon
  • 6 sides: Hexagon
  • 7 sides: Heptagon
  • 8 sides: Octagon
  • 9 sides: Nonagon
  • 10 sides: Decagon

Convex vs Concave Polygons

Convex polygon: All interior angles are less than 180°. Any line segment joining two vertices lies entirely inside the polygon.

Concave (non-convex) polygon: At least one interior angle is greater than 180° (a reflex angle). Part of a line segment joining two vertices may lie outside the polygon.

Regular polygon: All sides equal AND all angles equal (e.g. equilateral triangle, square).

Irregular polygon: Sides or angles are not all equal.

A polygon is also classified as simple (sides do not cross each other) or self-intersecting. In this course we study simple convex polygons.

Angle Sum Property of Polygons

The sum of all interior angles of a polygon depends on its number of sides.

Sum of interior angles of an n-sided polygon = (n - 2) × 180°

Derivation Idea

A polygon with n sides can be divided into (n - 2) triangles by drawing diagonals from one vertex. Each triangle has an angle sum of 180°, so the total is (n - 2) × 180°.

Interior Angle Sum Examples

  • Triangle (n=3): (3-2)×180° = 180°
  • Quadrilateral (n=4): (4-2)×180° = 360°
  • Pentagon (n=5): (5-2)×180° = 540°
  • Hexagon (n=6): (6-2)×180° = 720°
  • Octagon (n=8): (8-2)×180° = 1080°

Exterior Angle Sum

For any convex polygon, the sum of all exterior angles (one at each vertex) is always 360°, regardless of the number of sides.

Sum of exterior angles = 360°

For a regular n-sided polygon:

Each exterior angle = 360° / n
Each interior angle = 180° - (360°/n)

Example: Regular hexagon — each exterior angle = 360°/6 = 60°; each interior angle = 120°.

Types of Quadrilaterals

Quadrilaterals are classified based on their sides, angles, and diagonals. They form a hierarchy.

Trapezium

Exactly one pair of opposite sides is parallel (the parallel sides are called bases).

Parallelogram

Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel. (A parallelogram is a special trapezium.)

Rectangle

A parallelogram with all four angles equal to 90°.

Rhombus

A parallelogram with all four sides equal in length.

Square

A rectangle with all four sides equal (or a rhombus with all four angles 90°).

Kite

Two pairs of consecutive sides are equal (not opposite sides). The diagonals are perpendicular; one diagonal bisects the other.

Hierarchy Chart

  • Quadrilateral
  •   → Trapezium
  •   → Parallelogram
  •     → Rectangle
  •     → Rhombus
  •     → Square (is both rectangle and rhombus)
  •   → Kite

A square is the most special: it is a rectangle, a rhombus, a parallelogram, and a trapezium all at once.

Properties of a Parallelogram

A parallelogram (ABCD with AB || CD and AD || BC) has five key properties:

Sides and Angles

  • Opposite sides are equal: AB = CD and AD = BC
  • Opposite angles are equal: ∠A = ∠C and ∠B = ∠D
  • Consecutive angles are supplementary: ∠A + ∠B = 180°, ∠B + ∠C = 180°, etc.
∠A + ∠B = 180°   (co-interior angles)

Diagonals

  • Diagonals bisect each other: The diagonals of a parallelogram intersect at their midpoints. If diagonals AC and BD intersect at O, then AO = OC and BO = OD.
AO = OC   and   BO = OD

Note: In a general parallelogram the diagonals are NOT equal in length and do NOT bisect at right angles (that is special to rectangles and rhombuses respectively).

Converse Properties (sufficient conditions to prove a parallelogram)

  • If both pairs of opposite sides of a quadrilateral are equal, it is a parallelogram.
  • If both pairs of opposite angles are equal, it is a parallelogram.
  • If diagonals bisect each other, it is a parallelogram.
  • If one pair of opposite sides is both parallel and equal, it is a parallelogram.

Properties of Special Parallelograms

Rectangle

A parallelogram in which all angles are 90°.

  • Opposite sides are equal and parallel.
  • All four angles = 90°.
  • Diagonals are equal in length: AC = BD.
  • Diagonals bisect each other (but not at 90°).
Diagonal = √(l² + b²)

Rhombus

A parallelogram in which all four sides are equal.

  • All sides equal: AB = BC = CD = DA.
  • Opposite angles are equal.
  • Diagonals bisect each other at 90° (perpendicular bisectors of each other).
  • Each diagonal bisects the vertex angles.

Square

A square is both a rectangle and a rhombus — the most symmetric quadrilateral.

  • All four sides equal.
  • All four angles = 90°.
  • Diagonals are equal in length (like a rectangle).
  • Diagonals bisect each other at 90° (like a rhombus).
  • Each diagonal bisects the right angles, making 45° angles.
Diagonal of square = side × √2

Quick Comparison

  • Rectangle: diagonals equal, NOT perpendicular
  • Rhombus: diagonals perpendicular, NOT equal
  • Square: diagonals both equal AND perpendicular

Summary: Understanding Quadrilaterals

Angle Sum Formulas

Interior angle sum = (n - 2) × 180°
Exterior angle sum = 360° (any convex polygon)
Quadrilateral interior angle sum = 360°

Types of Quadrilaterals

  • Trapezium: 1 pair of parallel sides
  • Parallelogram: 2 pairs of parallel sides
  • Rectangle: parallelogram + all angles 90°
  • Rhombus: parallelogram + all sides equal
  • Square: rectangle + rhombus
  • Kite: 2 pairs of adjacent equal sides

Parallelogram Properties

  • Opposite sides equal
  • Opposite angles equal
  • Consecutive angles supplementary (sum = 180°)
  • Diagonals bisect each other

Diagonal Properties

  • Rectangle: diagonals equal length
  • Rhombus: diagonals perpendicular (90°)
  • Square: diagonals equal AND perpendicular
Square diagonal = side × √2
Rectangle diagonal = √(l² + b²)
