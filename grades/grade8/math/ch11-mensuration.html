Mensuration

Chapter 11 · Grade 8 Mathematics

The branch of mathematics that deals with the measurement of geometric figures — their lengths, areas, and volumes.

Topics covered: Area of trapezium & rhombus · Area of general polygons · Surface area of 3D shapes · Volume of 3D shapes · Unit conversions

Area of Trapezium and Rhombus

Trapezium

A trapezium has one pair of parallel sides (called parallel sides or bases) with lengths a and b, and a perpendicular height h between them.

Area of trapezium = ½ × (a + b) × h

Why? A trapezium can be split into a rectangle and two triangles, or seen as the average of the two parallel sides multiplied by the height.

Example: Parallel sides = 8 cm and 12 cm, height = 5 cm

Area = ½ × (8 + 12) × 5 = ½ × 20 × 5 = 50 cm²

Rhombus

A rhombus has all four sides equal. Its diagonals bisect each other at right angles. If the diagonals have lengths d₁ and d₂:

Area of rhombus = ½ × d₁ × d₂

Why? The diagonals divide the rhombus into 4 right-angled triangles. Total area = 4 × (½ × d₁/2 × d₂/2) = ½ d₁d₂.

Example: d₁ = 10 cm, d₂ = 6 cm

Area = ½ × 10 × 6 = 30 cm²

A square is a special rhombus where d₁ = d₂. For a square of side a: d₁ = d₂ = a√2, giving Area = ½ × a√2 × a√2 = a² ✓

Area of a General Polygon

Irregular polygons can be divided into triangles and trapezoids (trapeziums) whose areas are easy to calculate.

Triangulation Method

Draw diagonals from one vertex to split the polygon into triangles.

For an n-sided polygon: it splits into (n−2) triangles.

Example (Pentagon): 5 sides → 3 triangles

Calculate area of each triangle using ½ × base × height and add up.

Strip Method

For irregular shapes drawn on grid paper:

  • Count whole squares inside
  • Count half-squares (roughly ½ each)
  • Area ≈ full squares + ½ × boundary squares

Trapezoid-Strip Method (for fields)

Divide the polygon into vertical strips using a base line. Each strip is approximately a trapezium.

Measure heights at regular intervals (h₀, h₁, h₂, …hₙ) and use:

Area ≈ d × [(h₀+hₙ)/2 + h₁ + h₂ + … + hₙ₋₁]

where d is the uniform strip width.

Practical Example

A hexagonal plot: draw a rectangle around it, calculate the rectangle's area, then subtract the corner triangles that lie outside the hexagon.

All mensuration problems reduce to: identify the component shapes, find each area/volume, and add or subtract.

Surface Area of Cube, Cuboid, and Cylinder

Surface area = total area of all faces of a 3D solid. Think of it as the area of the net (unfolded shape).

Cube (side = a)

6 identical square faces, each with area a²

Total Surface Area = 6a²

Example: a = 5 cm → SA = 6 × 25 = 150 cm²

Cuboid (l × b × h)

3 pairs of rectangular faces:

  • Top + Bottom: 2 × lb
  • Front + Back: 2 × lh
  • Left + Right: 2 × bh

Total Surface Area = 2(lb + bh + lh)

Example: l=6, b=4, h=3 → SA = 2(24+12+18) = 2×54 = 108 cm²

Lateral Surface Area (excluding top & bottom): 2(l + b) × h

Cylinder (radius = r, height = h)

Components of surface:

  • 2 circular ends: 2 × πr²
  • Curved surface (roll out to rectangle: width = circumference = 2πr, height = h): 2πrh

Total Surface Area = 2πr² + 2πrh = 2πr(r + h)

Curved Surface Area = 2πrh

Example: r = 7 cm, h = 10 cm (use π = 22/7)

TSA = 2 × (22/7) × 7 × (7+10) = 2 × 22 × 17 = 748 cm²

For open cylinders (like a pipe), use only the curved surface area formula.

Volume of Cube, Cuboid, and Cylinder

Volume = amount of space occupied by a 3D solid. Measured in cubic units (cm³, m³, etc.).

Cube (side = a)

Volume = a³

Example: a = 4 cm → V = 64 cm³

Cuboid (l × b × h)

Volume = l × b × h

Example: l=8, b=5, h=3 → V = 120 cm³

Intuition: Volume = Base area × Height = (l × b) × h

Cylinder (radius = r, height = h)

Volume = πr²h

Intuition: Volume = (Area of circular base) × Height

Example: r = 3.5 cm, h = 10 cm

V = (22/7) × 3.5² × 10 = (22/7) × 12.25 × 10 = 385 cm³

Comparing Surface Area and Volume

Cube vs Cuboid: A cube uses the least surface area to enclose a given volume — that's why nature uses spheres and cubes efficiently.

Example: Which holds more — a cylinder with r=7, h=2 or r=3.5, h=8?

V₁ = π × 49 × 2 = 98π cm³

V₂ = π × 12.25 × 8 = 98π cm³

Equal volume! But their surface areas differ.

Applications

  • Volume of water a tank can hold
  • Amount of material needed to make a box
  • Comparing packing efficiency of different shapes

Volume vs Capacity; Unit Conversions

Volume vs Capacity

Volume: The total 3D space occupied by any object (solid or hollow). Measured in cm³, m³.

Capacity: The amount a hollow container can hold (liquid or gas). Measured in litres (L) or millilitres (mL).

1 mL = 1 cm³

1 L = 1000 cm³ = 1000 mL

1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³ = 1000 L = 1 kL

Example: A tank of volume 0.5 m³ has capacity 500 L.

Length and Area Unit Conversions

1 m = 100 cm ; 1 km = 1000 m

1 m² = 10,000 cm² (= 100 × 100)

1 km² = 1,000,000 m²

1 hectare (ha) = 10,000 m²

1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³ (= 100 × 100 × 100)

Worked Example

A cuboidal tank is 2 m long, 1.5 m wide, 80 cm deep. Find volume in litres.

80 cm = 0.8 m

V = 2 × 1.5 × 0.8 = 2.4 m³ = 2400 L

Always convert all units to the same system before calculating area or volume!
