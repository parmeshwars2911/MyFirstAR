Rational Numbers

Chapter 1 · Grade 8 Mathematics

In this chapter we explore the set of rational numbers — what they are, how they behave under arithmetic operations, and how to locate them on the number line. Rational numbers extend the integers and give us the ability to represent parts of a whole precisely.

What Are Rational Numbers?

A rational number is any number that can be expressed in the form p/q, where p and q are integers and q is not equal to zero.

Rational Number = p / q    where p, q ∈ Integers and q ≠ 0

Examples (Rational)

  • 1/2 — p = 1, q = 2
  • -3/4 — p = -3, q = 4
  • 5/1 = 5 — every integer is rational
  • 0/7 = 0 — zero is rational
  • -7/1 = -7 — negative integers are rational
  • 0.75 = 3/4 — terminating decimals are rational
  • 0.333... = 1/3 — repeating decimals are rational

Non-Examples (Irrational)

  • √2 ≈ 1.414... — non-terminating, non-repeating
  • π ≈ 3.14159... — non-terminating, non-repeating
  • √3, √5 — surds that cannot be written as p/q
  • e ≈ 2.71828... — Euler's number is irrational

Key insight: Every integer n is a rational number because n = n/1. All fractions whose numerator and denominator are integers (denominator non-zero) are rational. A decimal is rational if and only if it terminates or has a repeating block.

Properties of Rational Numbers

Rational numbers satisfy several important algebraic properties. Understanding which properties hold for which operations is essential.

Closure

The result of the operation on two rational numbers is also a rational number.

  • Addition: Closed — p/q + r/s = rational
  • Subtraction: Closed — p/q - r/s = rational
  • Multiplication: Closed — (p/q) × (r/s) = pr/qs, rational
  • Division: Closed (except dividing by 0)

Commutativity

Changing the order of operands does not change the result.

  • Addition: a + b = b + a (commutative)
  • Subtraction: a - b ≠ b - a (NOT commutative)
  • Multiplication: a × b = b × a (commutative)
  • Division: a ÷ b ≠ b ÷ a (NOT commutative)

Associativity

Grouping of operands does not change the result.

  • Addition: (a+b)+c = a+(b+c) (associative)
  • Subtraction: (a-b)-c ≠ a-(b-c) (NOT associative)
  • Multiplication: (a×b)×c = a×(b×c) (associative)
  • Division: (a÷b)÷c ≠ a÷(b÷c) (NOT associative)

Distributivity

Multiplication distributes over addition and subtraction.

a × (b + c) = a×b + a×c
a × (b - c) = a×b - a×c

This property holds for all rational numbers a, b, c.

Identities and Inverses

Special rational numbers play the role of identity elements and inverses under addition and multiplication.

Additive Identity

The number 0 is the additive identity. Adding 0 to any rational number leaves it unchanged.

a + 0 = 0 + a = a

Example: 3/4 + 0 = 3/4

Multiplicative Identity

The number 1 is the multiplicative identity. Multiplying any rational number by 1 leaves it unchanged.

a × 1 = 1 × a = a

Example: (-5/7) × 1 = -5/7

Additive Inverse

For every rational number a, there exists a rational number -a such that their sum is 0.

a + (-a) = 0

Example: 3/4 + (-3/4) = 0

The additive inverse of p/q is -p/q.

Multiplicative Inverse (Reciprocal)

For every non-zero rational number a = p/q, there exists 1/a = q/p such that their product is 1.

a × (1/a) = 1    (a ≠ 0)

Example: (3/4) × (4/3) = 12/12 = 1

Note: 0 has no multiplicative inverse.

Summary: 0 is unique as additive identity; 1 is unique as multiplicative identity. Every rational number has an additive inverse; every non-zero rational number has a multiplicative inverse.

Rational Numbers Between Two Rationals

One remarkable property of rational numbers is that they are dense: between any two distinct rational numbers, there are infinitely many other rational numbers. This is called the density property.

Between any two rationals a and b (a < b), there are infinitely many rationals.

Method 1: Finding the Mean

The simplest rational number between a and b is their arithmetic mean (average):

Mean = (a + b) / 2

Example: Find a rational number between 1/3 and 1/2.

Mean = (1/3 + 1/2) / 2 = (2/6 + 3/6) / 2 = (5/6) / 2 = 5/12

Check: 1/3 = 4/12, 5/12, 1/2 = 6/12 — yes, 5/12 lies between them.

Method 2: Using Equivalent Fractions

Convert both fractions to a common denominator and look for fractions in between.

Example: Rational numbers between 1/4 and 1/2.

1/4 = 10/40    and    1/2 = 20/40

Rationals in between: 11/40, 12/40, 13/40, ..., 19/40 — that is 9 rational numbers at this level alone. We can always find more by increasing the denominator.

Why Infinitely Many?

Applying the mean method repeatedly: take the mean of a and b, then take the mean of a and that result, and so on. This process never ends, producing an infinite sequence of distinct rationals all lying between a and b.

Representation on the Number Line

Every rational number corresponds to a unique point on the number line. The number line extends infinitely in both directions, with 0 at the center.

  • Positive rationals are located to the right of 0.
  • Negative rationals are located to the left of 0.
  • 0 itself is neither positive nor negative.

How to Plot p/q on the Number Line

  1. Identify the integers on either side of p/q. For example, for 3/4, the integers are 0 and 1.
  2. Divide the unit segment between those integers into q equal parts.
  3. Count p parts from the left integer. That point represents p/q.
To plot 3/4: divide segment [0, 1] into 4 equal parts; count 3 parts from 0.

Example: Plotting -5/3

  1. -5/3 lies between -2 and -1 (since -2 = -6/3 and -1 = -3/3).
  2. Divide the segment [-2, -1] into 3 equal parts.
  3. Count 2 parts to the right from -2 (since -5/3 = -2 + 1/3).

Mixed Numbers and Number Line

A rational number greater than 1 or less than -1 can be converted to a mixed number to identify which unit interval it falls in. For example, 7/3 = 2 + 1/3, so it lies between 2 and 3, one-third of the way from 2 to 3.

Key fact: Rational numbers are dense on the number line — between any two points representing rationals, there are infinitely many other rational points, yet there are still gaps occupied by irrational numbers like √2 and π.

Summary: Rational Numbers

Key Definition

Rational Number = p/q, q ≠ 0, p,q ∈ Z

Identities

a + 0 = a   (Additive Identity)
a × 1 = a   (Multiplicative Identity)

Inverses

a + (-a) = 0   (Additive Inverse)
a × (1/a) = 1   (Multiplicative Inverse, a ≠ 0)

Properties

  • Closure: holds for +, -, ×, ÷ (not by 0)
  • Commutativity: holds for + and × only
  • Associativity: holds for + and × only
  • Distributivity: × over + and -

Density Property

Mean of a and b = (a + b) / 2

Between any two rationals, there are infinitely many rationals.

Number Line

To plot p/q: divide the unit segment into q equal parts, count p parts from the integer to the left. Positive rationals are right of 0; negative rationals are left of 0.
