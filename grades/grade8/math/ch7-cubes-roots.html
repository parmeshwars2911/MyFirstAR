Cubes and Cube Roots

Chapter 7 · Grade 8 Mathematics

Just as squaring and finding square roots are inverse operations, cubing a number and finding its cube root are inverses. Cubes appear in volume calculations (volume of a cube = side cubed) and in various number patterns. The Hardy-Ramanujan number 1729 is a famous example of the mathematical beauty hidden in cubes.

Perfect Cubes and the Hardy-Ramanujan Number

The cube of a number n is n × n × n = n³. A perfect cube is the cube of a whole number.

Perfect Cubes (1 to 10)

  • 1³ = 1
  • 2³ = 8
  • 3³ = 27
  • 4³ = 64
  • 5³ = 125
  • 6³ = 216
  • 7³ = 343
  • 8³ = 512
  • 9³ = 729
  • 10³ = 1000

The Hardy-Ramanujan Number: 1729

The mathematician Srinivasa Ramanujan once told G.H. Hardy that 1729 is a remarkable number. Hardy had arrived in a taxi numbered 1729 and thought it was dull.

1729 is the smallest number expressible as the sum of two positive cubes in two different ways:

1729 = 1³ + 12³ = 1 + 1728
1729 = 9³ + 10³ = 729 + 1000

Such numbers are called taxicab numbers. 1729 is also known as the Hardy-Ramanujan number in honour of this story.

Properties of Cubes

Parity and Sign Properties

  • The cube of an even number is always even: (2n)³ = 8n³
  • The cube of an odd number is always odd
  • The cube of a positive number is positive
  • The cube of a negative number is negative
(-3)³ = -27    (-5)³ = -125

This differs from squares: squares of negatives are always positive, but cubes of negatives are always negative.

Sum Identity

1³ + 2³ + 3³ + ... + n³ = [n(n+1)/2]²

Unit Digit Pattern of Cubes

The unit digit of n³ depends only on the unit digit of n:

  • Unit digit 0 → cube ends in 0
  • Unit digit 1 → cube ends in 1
  • Unit digit 2 → cube ends in 8
  • Unit digit 3 → cube ends in 7
  • Unit digit 4 → cube ends in 4
  • Unit digit 5 → cube ends in 5
  • Unit digit 6 → cube ends in 6
  • Unit digit 7 → cube ends in 3
  • Unit digit 8 → cube ends in 2
  • Unit digit 9 → cube ends in 9

Key pairs: 2 and 8 swap; 3 and 7 swap; 0,1,4,5,6,9 cube to themselves.

Cube Root by Prime Factorisation

To find the cube root of a perfect cube using prime factorisation:

  1. Express the number as a product of its prime factors.
  2. Arrange the prime factors in groups of three (triples).
  3. Take one factor from each triple.
  4. Multiply the selected factors — the product is the cube root.
∛(p³ × q³ × r³) = p × q × r

Example 1: ∛216

216 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 27
      = 2³ × 3³
∛216 = 2 × 3 = 6

Verify: 6³ = 216 ✓

Example 2: ∛3375

3375 = 3 × 3 × 3 × 125
       = 3³ × 5³
∛3375 = 3 × 5 = 15

Verify: 15³ = 3375 ✓

Checking Perfect Cubes

In the prime factorisation of a perfect cube, every prime must appear exactly a multiple of 3 times. If any prime appears 1 or 2 times, the number is NOT a perfect cube.

Example: 72 = 2³ × 3² — the factor 3 appears only twice, so 72 is not a perfect cube.

Estimation of Cube Roots

For large perfect cubes (up to 6 digits), we can find the cube root by grouping digits and using the unit digit pattern — without full prime factorisation.

Method

  1. Group the digits in sets of three from the right. A 6-digit number gives 2 groups; a 5-digit number also gives 2 groups (first group may have only 1 or 2 digits).
  2. Right group: The unit digit of this group reveals the unit digit of the cube root using the unit digit pattern.
  3. Left group: Find n such that n³ ≤ left group < (n+1)³. This n is the tens digit of the cube root.

Example 1: ∛74088

Groups: 74 | 088
Right group ends in 8 → cube root ends in 2
Left group = 74: 4³=64 ≤ 74 < 125=5³ → tens digit = 4
∛74088 = 42

Verify: 42³ = 74088 ✓

Example 2: ∛17576

Groups: 17 | 576
Right group ends in 6 → cube root ends in 6
Left group = 17: 2³=8 ≤ 17 < 27=3³ → tens digit = 2
∛17576 = 26

Verify: 26³ = 17576 ✓

Cube Roots of Negative Numbers and Fractions

Cube Roots of Negative Numbers

Unlike square roots, cube roots of negative numbers are real numbers (and negative).

∛(-a) = -∛(a)

This follows because (-n)³ = -n³, so the cube root reverses the negative sign.

Examples:

  • ∛(-8) = -2   (since (-2)³ = -8)
  • ∛(-27) = -3   (since (-3)³ = -27)
  • ∛(-125) = -5   (since (-5)³ = -125)
  • ∛(-1000) = -10
  • ∛(-343) = -7   (since (-7)³ = -343)

Key difference from square roots: √(-4) is not a real number, but ∛(-8) = -2 is a real number.

Cube Roots of Fractions

∛(p/q) = ∛p / ∛q

Find the cube root of numerator and denominator separately.

Examples:

  • ∛(8/27) = ∛8 / ∛27 = 2/3
  • ∛(64/125) = ∛64 / ∛125 = 4/5
  • ∛(1/216) = 1/6
  • ∛(-27/343) = -3/7

Mixed number example:

∛(3 and 3/8) = ∛(27/8) = 3/2 = 1.5
