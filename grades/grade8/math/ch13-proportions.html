Direct and Inverse Proportions

Chapter 13 · Grade 8 Mathematics

When two quantities are related, a change in one causes a predictable change in the other — either in the same direction or in the opposite direction.

Topics covered: Direct proportion · Inverse proportion · Real-life examples · Unitary method · Chain rule

Direct Proportion

Two quantities x and y are in direct proportion if, when x increases, y increases by the same ratio — and vice versa.

Definition and Formula

x/y = k (constant) ⟺ x ∝ y

Also written as: x₁/y₁ = x₂/y₂

or: x₁ × y₂ = x₂ × y₁

The constant k is called the constant of proportionality.

Identifying Direct Proportion

Check: Does doubling x double y? Does halving x halve y?

If yes → direct proportion. The ratio x/y must remain constant throughout.

On a graph: direct proportion gives a straight line through the origin.

Example

A car travels at uniform speed. In 3 hours it covers 180 km. How far in 5 hours?

Distance and time are in direct proportion (speed constant).

180/3 = d/5

d = 180 × 5 / 3 = 300 km

Direct Proportion — More Examples

Cost and Quantity

If 5 kg of rice costs ₹200, what is the cost of 8 kg?

Cost ∝ Quantity (same price per kg)

200/5 = C/8

C = 200 × 8 / 5 = ₹320

Shadow Length and Height

A 6 m tall pole casts a 4 m shadow. A building casts a 20 m shadow. Find the building's height.

Height ∝ Shadow length (same sun angle)

6/4 = H/20

H = 6 × 20/4 = 30 m

Map Scale

If 1 cm on a map = 5 km in reality, what actual distance does 3.5 cm represent?

Map distance ∝ Actual distance

1/5 = 3.5/d

d = 3.5 × 5 = 17.5 km

Wages and Work Days

A worker earns ₹450 in 3 days. How much in 7 days?

450/3 = W/7

W = 450 × 7/3 = ₹1050

Key question: If x doubles, does y also double? If YES → Direct Proportion. If NO → check for inverse proportion.

Inverse Proportion

Two quantities x and y are in inverse proportion if, when x increases, y decreases proportionally — their product stays constant.

Definition and Formula

x × y = k (constant) ⟺ x ∝ 1/y

Also written as: x₁ × y₁ = x₂ × y₂

or: x₁/x₂ = y₂/y₁ (ratios are inverted!)

Identifying Inverse Proportion

Check: Does doubling x halve y? Does tripling x reduce y to one-third?

If yes → inverse proportion. The product x × y must remain constant.

On a graph: inverse proportion gives a hyperbola (not a straight line).

Example

6 workers can complete a job in 8 days. How long will 12 workers take?

More workers → fewer days (inverse proportion)

6 × 8 = 12 × d

d = 48/12 = 4 days

Inverse Proportion — More Examples

Speed and Time

A train covers a fixed distance in 3 hours at 80 km/h. How long at 120 km/h?

Speed × Time = Distance (constant)

80 × 3 = 120 × t

t = 240/120 = 2 hours

Pipes Filling a Tank

1 pipe fills a tank in 12 hours. How long do 3 pipes take (same rate each)?

More pipes → less time (inverse)

1 × 12 = 3 × t

t = 12/3 = 4 hours

Gear and Rotations

A gear with 24 teeth meshes with one having 8 teeth. If the first rotates 40 times/min, how fast does the second rotate?

Teeth × Rotations = constant (same chain speed)

24 × 40 = 8 × r

r = 960/8 = 120 rotations/min

Wavelength and Frequency

Speed of wave = wavelength × frequency (constant for a medium)

So wavelength ∝ 1/frequency — a classic inverse proportion in physics!

In inverse proportion, both quantities cannot be 0 (since their product must equal a non-zero constant k).

Chain Rule and Unitary Method

Unitary Method

Find the value of one unit, then scale up or down.

Direct example: 15 books cost ₹525. Cost of 8 books?

1 book = 525/15 = ₹35

8 books = 35 × 8 = ₹280

Inverse example: 1 worker takes 48 days. How many days for 6 workers?

Total work = 48 worker-days

6 workers → 48/6 = 8 days

Chain Rule (Compound Proportion)

When a problem involves more than two quantities linked together, use the chain rule.

Example: 10 men can build a wall 50 m long in 20 days working 8 hours/day. How many days for 15 men to build a 75 m wall working 6 hours/day?

Identify each proportion type:

  • Men: 10→15, more men → fewer days (inverse)
  • Length: 50→75, longer wall → more days (direct)
  • Hours/day: 8→6, fewer hours/day → more days (inverse)

Days = 20 × (10/15) × (75/50) × (8/6)

= 20 × (2/3) × (3/2) × (4/3) = 20 × 4/3 ≈ 26.67 days

For chain rule: multiply by factor < 1 for inverse proportion; multiply by factor > 1 for direct proportion.

Chapter Summary – Direct and Inverse Proportions

Direct Proportion

x/y = k (constant)

x₁/y₁ = x₂/y₂

  • x increases → y increases (same ratio)
  • Graph: straight line through origin
  • Examples: cost & quantity, distance & time (constant speed), shadow length & height

Inverse Proportion

x × y = k (constant)

x₁ × y₁ = x₂ × y₂

  • x increases → y decreases (product constant)
  • Graph: hyperbola
  • Examples: speed & time, workers & days, pipes & filling time

Methods

Ratio method: Set up equal ratios and solve for unknown.

Unitary method: Find value per unit, then scale.

Chain rule: For multiple linked quantities — identify each as direct or inverse and multiply appropriate fractions.

Quick Decision Guide

Ask: "If I increase one quantity, does the other increase (→ direct) or decrease (→ inverse)?"

Direct: same direction

Inverse: opposite direction
