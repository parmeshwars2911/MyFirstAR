Practical Geometry

Chapter 4 · Grade 8 Mathematics

Practical geometry involves the construction of geometric figures using compass and ruler. In this chapter we focus on constructing quadrilaterals under various given conditions, understanding why exactly 5 independent measurements are needed, and handling special cases like squares, rectangles, and rhombuses.

Why Five Measurements?

A quadrilateral ABCD has the following measurable elements:

  • 4 sides: AB, BC, CD, DA
  • 4 angles: ∠A, ∠B, ∠C, ∠D
  • 2 diagonals: AC and BD

That is a total of 10 measurements. However, these are not all independent — the four angles always sum to 360° (so knowing three determines the fourth), and various other relationships exist. A unique quadrilateral can be determined with exactly 5 independent measurements.

10 total measurements − dependencies = 5 independent measurements needed

Key Insight: Triangles Within Quadrilaterals

A diagonal divides a quadrilateral into two triangles. Since a triangle is uniquely determined by 3 measurements (like SSS, SAS, ASA), two triangles sharing a diagonal need 3 + 3 = 6 measurements, minus the 1 shared diagonal = 5 measurements total.

Possible Combinations of 5 Measurements

  • 4 sides + 1 diagonal
  • 3 sides + 2 diagonals
  • 4 sides + 1 angle
  • 3 sides + 2 angles (angles included between given sides)
  • Special shapes may need fewer due to extra constraints

Case 1: Four Sides and One Diagonal

Given: All four sides (AB, BC, CD, DA) and one diagonal (say AC).

Construction Steps

The diagonal divides the quadrilateral into two triangles: triangle ABC and triangle ACD.

  1. Draw diagonal AC to the given length.
  2. Construct triangle ABC: With A as centre draw an arc of radius AB; with C as centre draw an arc of radius BC. Their intersection is point B.
  3. Construct triangle ACD: With A as centre draw an arc of radius AD; with C as centre draw an arc of radius CD. Their intersection is point D (on the opposite side of AC from B).
  4. Join AB, BC, CD, DA to complete quadrilateral ABCD.
Quadrilateral ABCD = Triangle ABC + Triangle ACD (sharing diagonal AC)

Example

Construct quadrilateral ABCD where AB = 4 cm, BC = 3 cm, CD = 5 cm, DA = 4 cm, and diagonal AC = 6 cm.

  • Draw AC = 6 cm.
  • Draw triangle ABC with AB = 4 cm, BC = 3 cm.
  • Draw triangle ACD with AD = 4 cm, CD = 5 cm on opposite side of AC.

Note: The two triangles must be on opposite sides of the diagonal for a valid quadrilateral.

Case 2: Three Sides and Two Diagonals

Given: Three consecutive sides (AB, BC, CD) and both diagonals (AC and BD).

Construction Steps

  1. Draw triangle ABC using the three measurements AB, BC, and diagonal AC (SSS construction).
  2. Locate vertex D: From A, draw an arc of radius AD (which equals the fourth side DA — wait: here we have only 3 sides). Actually: from B draw arc = BD; from C draw arc = CD. Their intersection gives D.
  3. Specifically: with B as centre and radius BD, draw an arc. With C as centre and radius CD, draw an arc. Where they intersect (on the correct side) is D.
  4. Join AD and CD to complete the quadrilateral.
Given: AB, BC, CD, diagonal AC, diagonal BD → construct triangle ABC first, then locate D

Example

Construct quadrilateral PQRS: PQ = 4.5 cm, QR = 3.5 cm, RS = 5 cm, diagonal PR = 6 cm, diagonal QS = 5.5 cm.

  1. Draw triangle PQR using PQ = 4.5 cm, QR = 3.5 cm, PR = 6 cm (SSS).
  2. With Q as centre, draw arc of radius QS = 5.5 cm. With R as centre, draw arc of radius RS = 5 cm. Their intersection is S.
  3. Join PS and RS to complete quadrilateral PQRS.

Case 3: Four Sides and One Angle; Case 4: Three Sides and Two Angles

Case 3: Four Sides + One Angle

Given: AB, BC, CD, DA, and angle B.

  1. Draw AB to the given length.
  2. Construct angle B at point B.
  3. Mark BC along the arm of angle B.
  4. Draw arcs from A (radius = AD) and from C (radius = CD). Their intersection is D.
  5. Join AD and CD.
Start with side AB, construct ∠B, mark BC, then locate D using arcs from A and C.

Example: ABCD with AB = 5 cm, BC = 4 cm, CD = 4 cm, DA = 5 cm, ∠B = 75°.

Case 4: Three Sides + Two Angles

Given: AB, BC, CD, and angles B and C (angles between the given sides).

  1. Draw AB to the given length.
  2. Construct angle B at B and mark BC on its arm.
  3. Construct angle C at C and mark CD on its arm.
  4. Point D is the end of segment CD. Join AD to close the quadrilateral.
Construct ∠B at B and ∠C at C; the intersection of arms gives sides and D.

Note: The two given angles must be adjacent (at connected vertices) and formed between the given sides for the construction to work.

Special Cases: Rectangle, Square, Rhombus

For special quadrilaterals, extra properties reduce the number of independent measurements needed below 5.

Constructing a Rectangle

A rectangle has all angles = 90° and opposite sides equal. You only need length and breadth.

  1. Draw AB = length (l).
  2. At A, construct a 90° angle. Mark AD = breadth (b).
  3. At B, construct a 90° angle. Mark BC = breadth (b).
  4. Join DC. (DC should equal l automatically.)
Rectangle: given l and b, all angles = 90°

Constructing a Square

A square has all sides equal and all angles = 90°. You only need one side.

  1. Draw AB = side (a).
  2. At A and B, construct 90° angles.
  3. Mark AD = BC = a. Join DC.
Square: given one side a, all sides = a, all angles = 90°

Constructing a Rhombus

A rhombus has all sides equal. You need either one side and one angle OR one side and one diagonal.

Method 1 (side + angle):

  1. Draw AB = side (a).
  2. At A, construct the given angle ∠A. Mark AD = a along the arm.
  3. With B as centre (radius a) and D as centre (radius a), mark C.

Method 2 (side + diagonal):

  1. Draw diagonal AC.
  2. With A and C as centres and radius = side, draw arcs. Intersections give B and D.
  3. Join AB, BC, CD, DA.
Rhombus: all 4 sides = a; diagonals bisect at 90°

Summary: Practical Geometry

Five Measurements Required

A unique quadrilateral requires exactly 5 independent measurements.

Construction Cases

  • 4 sides + 1 diagonal: Draw diagonal, construct two triangles
  • 3 sides + 2 diagonals: Construct triangle from 2 sides + diagonal, locate 4th vertex using 2nd diagonal and remaining side
  • 4 sides + 1 angle: Construct the angle first, mark sides, locate remaining vertex by arcs
  • 3 sides + 2 angles: Construct both angles at their vertices, mark sides along arms

Special Constructions

  • Rectangle: length + breadth (all angles = 90°)
  • Square: one side only (all sides equal, all angles = 90°)
  • Rhombus: side + angle OR side + diagonal (all sides equal)

General Tips

  • Always draw a rough sketch before starting the accurate construction
  • Label all points clearly with capital letters
  • Check that the constructed figure satisfies all given measurements
  • Use a sharp pencil for accuracy
  • Verify by measuring the unknown sides or angles after construction
