Materials: Metals and Non-Metals

Chapter 4 · Grade 8 Science

All materials around us — from the iron in a bridge to the carbon in a pencil — can be classified as metals or non-metals based on their physical and chemical properties.

Topics covered: Physical properties of metals · Physical properties of non-metals · Chemical reactions · Uses of metals and non-metals

Physical Properties of Metals

Most elements in the periodic table are metals. Metals share a distinctive set of physical characteristics.

  • Lustre: Metals have a shiny surface when freshly cut or polished (e.g., gold, silver, copper). This property makes them useful for jewellery and decorations.
  • Hardness: Most metals are hard (e.g., iron, steel). Exception: sodium and potassium are very soft and can be cut with a knife.
  • Malleability: Metals can be beaten into thin sheets without breaking. Gold is the most malleable metal — can be beaten into gold leaf just a few atoms thick.
  • Ductility: Metals can be drawn into thin wires. Copper and aluminium are used in electrical wiring because of this property.
  • Conductivity: Metals are good conductors of heat and electricity. Silver is the best conductor; copper and aluminium are widely used in wires.
  • Sonorous: Metals produce a ringing sound when struck (used for bells, cymbals).
  • High melting/boiling points: Most metals have high melting points. Exception: Mercury (Hg) is liquid at room temperature.
  • High density: Generally heavier than non-metals. Exception: Sodium and potassium are lighter than water and float on it.

The most malleable metal is gold; the most ductile metal is also gold. Silver is the best conductor of electricity.

Physical Properties of Non-Metals

Non-metals have properties that are generally opposite to those of metals.

Typical Properties

  • Brittle: Solid non-metals break into pieces when hammered (e.g., sulphur, phosphorus, iodine)
  • Non-lustrous: Generally dull-looking; cannot be polished to a shine
  • Poor conductors: Do not conduct heat or electricity well — used as insulators
  • Low density: Generally lighter than metals
  • Low melting/boiling points: Many are gases at room temperature (O2, N2, Cl2, F2)
  • Not sonorous: Do not ring when struck
  • Not malleable/ductile: Solid non-metals crumble when beaten

Important Exceptions

  • Graphite (form of carbon): Non-metal that conducts electricity — used in pencils and as electrodes
  • Iodine: Non-metal with metallic lustre
  • Diamond (carbon): Hardest natural substance — but still a non-metal
  • Bromine: Only liquid non-metal at room temperature

There are only 22 non-metals but they include the most important elements for life: Carbon, Oxygen, Hydrogen, Nitrogen.

Chemical Properties of Metals

Metals undergo characteristic chemical reactions with air, water, and acids.

  • Reaction with Air (Oxygen):
    Metal + Oxygen → Metal oxide (basic in nature)
    • Iron rusts in moist air: 4Fe + 3O2 + water → Fe2O3·H2O (rust)
    • Copper turns green: 2Cu + O2 → 2CuO (black), then forms copper carbonate (green patina)
    • Aluminium forms a protective oxide layer that prevents further corrosion
    • Gold and silver: Do not react with oxygen — hence used for jewellery
  • Reaction with Water:
    • Sodium/Potassium: React vigorously with cold water, producing hydrogen gas and heat
    • Iron: Reacts slowly with water and air (rusting)
    • Gold, silver, copper: Do not react with water
    2Na + 2H2O → 2NaOH + H2↑
  • Reaction with Acids:
    Metal + Dilute acid → Salt + Hydrogen gas

    Zinc + Dilute H2SO4 → Zinc sulphate + H2↑. Copper and silver do not react with dilute HCl or H2SO4.

  • Displacement reactions: A more reactive metal displaces a less reactive metal from its salt solution (e.g., iron displaces copper from copper sulphate solution).

Chemical Properties of Non-Metals

Non-metals react with oxygen and other elements in characteristic ways different from metals.

Reaction with Oxygen

Non-metal + Oxygen → Non-metal oxide (acidic in nature)
  • Carbon + O2 → CO2 (carbon dioxide) — acidic
  • Sulphur + O2 → SO2 (sulphur dioxide) — acidic, causes acid rain
  • Phosphorus + O2 → P4O10
  • Non-metal oxides dissolve in water to form acids:
    CO2 + H2O → H2CO3 (carbonic acid)

Metal oxides are basic; non-metal oxides are acidic — this is a key distinguishing reaction.

Reaction with Hydrogen and Other Properties

  • Non-metals react with hydrogen to form compounds like water, hydrogen chloride:
    H2 + Cl2 → 2HCl
    H2 + S → H2S
  • Non-metals do not displace hydrogen from acids
  • Non-metals do not react with water (except halogen reactions with water)
  • Non-metals generally have lower reactivity with common reagents compared to metals

Chlorine (non-metal) is used to purify drinking water because it kills bacteria.

Uses of Metals and Non-Metals

Uses of Metals

  • Iron and Steel: Construction (bridges, buildings, railways), machinery, vehicles
  • Copper: Electrical wires, cooking utensils, plumbing
  • Aluminium: Aircraft, kitchen foil, electrical cables, food cans
  • Gold and Silver: Jewellery, electronic components, coins
  • Zinc: Galvanising iron (rust protection), batteries, die casting
  • Mercury: Thermometers, barometers, fluorescent lamps
  • Titanium: Aerospace, medical implants (strong and lightweight)
  • Lead: Batteries, radiation shielding

Uses of Non-Metals

  • Oxygen: Respiration, hospitals, welding, rocket fuel
  • Nitrogen: Fertilizers, preserving food, liquid nitrogen for cooling
  • Carbon: Diamond (cutting tools, jewellery), graphite (pencils, electrodes), coal (fuel)
  • Sulphur: Vulcanising rubber, making gunpowder, medicines
  • Chlorine: Water purification, PVC, bleach
  • Phosphorus: Fertilizers, matches, fireworks
  • Silicon: Computer chips, semiconductors, glass
  • Iodine: Antiseptic, thyroid medication

Chapter Summary

Key Concepts

  • Metals: lustrous, malleable, ductile, good conductors, sonorous
  • Non-metals: brittle, non-lustrous, poor conductors (exception: graphite)
  • Metal oxides are basic; non-metal oxides are acidic
  • Metals react with acid to give salt + hydrogen gas
  • More reactive metals displace less reactive ones from solutions
  • Gold and platinum are least reactive metals (noble metals)

Key Terms

  • Malleability: Can be beaten into sheets
  • Ductility: Can be drawn into wires
  • Lustre: Shiny appearance of metals
  • Corrosion: Slow destruction of metals (rusting)
  • Displacement reaction: Reactive metal pushes out less reactive one
  • Galvanising: Coating iron with zinc to prevent rust
  • Alloy: Mixture of two or more metals (e.g., brass, steel)

Understanding metal and non-metal properties helps us choose the right material for every application — from bridges to batteries to medicines.
