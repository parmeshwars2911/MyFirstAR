Chapter 15

Some Natural Phenomena

Understanding lightning, earthquakes, and cyclones — nature's most powerful events and how to stay safe

Grade 8 Science

Lightning

Lightning is a massive electrical discharge (spark) between oppositely charged regions of clouds, or between a cloud and the earth's surface.

How Lightning is Caused

  • Inside large storm clouds (cumulonimbus), strong winds cause water droplets and ice crystals to collide and rub against each other
  • This friction causes charge separation: positive charges accumulate at the top of the cloud, negative charges accumulate at the bottom
  • The negative charges at the bottom of the cloud induce positive charges on the ground below
  • When the charge builds up sufficiently, a massive electric discharge occurs — this is lightning
  • The lightning bolt heats the air around it to about 30,000°C, causing the air to expand rapidly, creating the loud sound we hear as thunder
  • We see lightning before we hear thunder because light travels much faster than sound

Lightning Conductor

A lightning conductor (or lightning rod) is a tall metal rod installed at the top of tall buildings, connected to the earth by a thick copper wire. It provides a safe path for lightning current to flow into the ground, protecting the building from damage.
  • Invented by Benjamin Franklin in 1752
  • The pointed tip attracts lightning discharge and channels it safely to the ground

Earthquakes

An earthquake is a sudden shaking or trembling of the Earth's surface caused by the movement of tectonic plates or volcanic activity.

Cause of Earthquakes

  • The Earth's crust is broken into large pieces called tectonic plates that float on the molten mantle
  • These plates are constantly moving (very slowly, a few centimetres per year)
  • At boundaries called fault lines, plates can collide, slide past each other, or separate — this sudden movement releases enormous energy
  • Focus (Hypocenter): The point inside the Earth where the earthquake originates
  • Epicenter: The point on the Earth's surface directly above the focus — shaking is most severe here

Seismic Waves

  • P-waves (Primary waves): Longitudinal waves; travel through solids, liquids, and gases; arrive first
  • S-waves (Secondary waves): Transverse waves; travel only through solids; arrive second
  • Surface waves: Travel along the Earth's surface; cause the most destruction
Richter Scale: Measures the magnitude of an earthquake on a logarithmic scale (1-10). Each unit represents 10 times more ground motion and about 31.6 times more energy. Earthquakes above magnitude 7 are considered major.

Seismograph and Earthquake Zones in India

Seismograph

A seismograph is an instrument used to detect, measure, and record seismic waves (vibrations caused by earthquakes).
  • Principle: Based on the principle of inertia — a heavy pendulum remains stationary while the Earth (and the recording drum) moves beneath it
  • The relative movement between the stationary pendulum and the moving drum traces a wavy line on paper or a digital record called a seismogram
  • Scientists use seismograms to determine the location, depth, and magnitude of earthquakes
  • By comparing seismograms from multiple stations, the epicenter can be triangulated

Earthquake Zones in India

India is divided into seismic zones II to V based on earthquake risk (Zone V being the most hazardous):

  • Zone V (Highest risk): Northeast India (Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya), Jammu & Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Andaman & Nicobar Islands, parts of Gujarat (Kutch)
  • Zone IV: Parts of Himalayas, Delhi, Jammu, and surrounding regions
  • Zone III: Large parts of the Indo-Gangetic plain, parts of peninsular India
  • Zone II (Lowest risk): Parts of Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, and southern peninsula

Cyclones

A cyclone is a large-scale rotating storm system with a low-pressure center. It forms over warm tropical oceans and brings violent winds, heavy rainfall, and storm surges.

Formation of Cyclones

  • Warm, moist air over warm tropical oceans (above ~26°C) rises rapidly due to heating
  • This creates a region of very low air pressure at the surface
  • Cooler surrounding air rushes in to fill the low-pressure area, and as the Earth rotates, this air begins to spiral inward (anticlockwise in Northern Hemisphere, clockwise in Southern Hemisphere — Coriolis effect)
  • Rising air cools and condenses, releasing latent heat that further fuels the storm
  • A calm, clear region called the eye of the cyclone forms at the centre

Effects of Cyclones

  • Very high winds (can exceed 200 km/h)
  • Torrential rainfall and flooding
  • Storm surge: A rise in sea level that can inundate coastal areas
  • Destruction of buildings, crops, and infrastructure
  • Loss of life and displacement of people

Beaufort Scale

  • Measures wind speed from 0 to 12
  • 0 = Calm (less than 1 km/h)
  • 6 = Strong breeze (39–49 km/h)
  • 12 = Hurricane force (above 118 km/h)
  • Cyclone-force winds are typically Force 10 or above
  • India uses satellite-based cyclone tracking and early warning systems to alert coastal communities

Safety Measures During Natural Disasters

During Lightning

  • Stay indoors in a sturdy building or car
  • Avoid tall trees, open fields, hilltops, and exposed ridges
  • Stay away from water bodies (lakes, ponds, swimming pools)
  • Do not hold metal objects (umbrellas, golf clubs)
  • If outdoors with no shelter, squat low (do not lie flat) with feet together and hands over ears
  • Unplug electrical appliances to avoid surge damage

During Earthquake

  • Indoors: Take cover under a sturdy table or desk; hold on until shaking stops; stay away from windows, exterior walls, and heavy furniture
  • Outdoors: Move to open ground away from buildings, trees, and power lines; drop to hands and knees
  • Do NOT run out of buildings during shaking (risk of falling debris)
  • After shaking stops, check for gas leaks, fires, and injuries

During Cyclone

  • Heed official warnings and evacuate early if instructed
  • Move to designated cyclone shelters or inland areas
  • Secure or bring in loose objects that could become dangerous projectiles
  • Stock emergency supplies: water, non-perishable food, first aid kit, torch, radio, medications
  • Stay indoors during the cyclone; do not go outside during the calm eye (storm will resume)
  • Avoid flooded roads and bridges — water may be deeper than it appears

General Preparedness

  • Know your local disaster evacuation routes
  • Keep emergency contact numbers handy
  • Practice earthquake drills at school and home
  • Build earthquake-resistant structures in high-risk zones

Chapter 15 Summary: Some Natural Phenomena

  • Lightning: Electrical discharge between cloud and ground/clouds due to charge separation; negative charges build at cloud base; lightning conductor (metal rod + earthing) protects buildings
  • Thunder: Sound produced by rapid heating and expansion of air along lightning channel; heard after lightning because sound is slower than light
  • Earthquakes: Caused by sudden movement of tectonic plates at fault lines; focus = origin inside Earth; epicenter = point on surface above focus
  • Seismic waves: P-waves (longitudinal, travel through all media), S-waves (transverse, solids only), surface waves (most destructive)
  • Richter Scale: Logarithmic scale measuring earthquake magnitude; each unit = 10× more amplitude
  • Seismograph: Pendulum-based instrument detecting and recording seismic waves; seismogram is the record
  • India's seismic zones: Zone II (lowest) to Zone V (highest) — northeast India, J&K, Andaman & Nicobar are Zone V
  • Cyclones: Rotating storms with low-pressure centers; form over warm oceans; bring high winds, heavy rain, storm surge
  • Beaufort scale: Measures wind speed 0–12; cyclone-force is 10+
  • Safety: Lightning → indoors, away from trees/metal; Earthquake → under table, open ground; Cyclone → evacuate early, cyclone shelters
