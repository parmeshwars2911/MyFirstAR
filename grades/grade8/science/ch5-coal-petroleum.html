Coal and Petroleum

Chapter 5 · Grade 8 Science

Coal and petroleum are the fuels that power much of our modern world — from electricity to transport to plastics. Yet these resources took millions of years to form and will run out within a few centuries if we continue using them at the current rate.

Topics covered: Fossil fuels formation · Coal types and products · Petroleum refining · Petroleum products · Conservation

Fossil Fuels – Formation

Fossil fuels — coal, petroleum, and natural gas — are formed from the remains of dead organisms buried under layers of earth over millions of years. They are classified as non-renewable resources.

Formation Process

  • Step 1: Ancient plants, animals, and microorganisms died and accumulated in swamps, forests, and sea beds.
  • Step 2: Their remains were buried under sediment (sand, mud, rock) over millions of years.
  • Step 3: High pressure and heat (without oxygen) transformed organic matter into coal (from plants) or petroleum/natural gas (from marine organisms).
  • Time required: 300–400 million years. This is why they cannot be replenished on a human timescale.

Why They Are Exhaustible

  • Earth's reserves are finite and fixed
  • Current consumption rate far exceeds any natural renewal
  • Once burned, the energy released cannot be recovered
  • Estimated reserves: coal ~200 years, petroleum ~50 years at current consumption

Fossil fuels provide about 80% of the world's energy today. Their combustion is the primary driver of climate change.

Coal: Types and Products

Coal is the most abundant fossil fuel. It forms from dead vegetation compressed over millions of years (a process called carbonisation).

Types of Coal (in order of carbon content)

  • Peat: Youngest form; high moisture, lowest carbon content (~60%). Burns poorly, used as fertilizer.
  • Lignite (Brown coal): More compressed than peat; ~70% carbon. Poor quality fuel.
  • Bituminous coal: Most common type; ~80% carbon. Widely used in power plants and as cooking fuel.
  • Anthracite: Hardest, oldest form; ~90–95% carbon. Burns hottest and cleanest. Best quality coal.

Products from Coal (Destructive Distillation)

When coal is heated in the absence of air:

  • Coke: Almost pure carbon. Used as a reducing agent in iron smelting (blast furnace) and as a smokeless fuel.
  • Coal tar: Black, thick liquid. Mixture of ~200 chemicals. Used for making dyes, drugs, explosives, perfumes, plastics, naphthalene balls.
  • Coal gas: Mainly hydrogen, methane, CO. Excellent fuel with high calorific value. Used for streetlights historically.
  • Ammoniacal liquor: Used for making fertilizers.

Coal is also known as "black gold." India has large coal reserves, especially in Jharkhand, Odisha, and West Bengal.

Petroleum: Formation and Refining

Petroleum (from Latin: "petra" = rock, "oleum" = oil) is a dark oily liquid found deep underground between layers of rock.

Formation

  • Formed from tiny marine organisms (plankton, algae) that died and sank to the ocean floor
  • Covered by sediment and subjected to heat and pressure for millions of years
  • Organic matter converted to liquid petroleum trapped in porous rock layers
  • Natural gas (mainly methane) forms above petroleum deposits
  • Extracted by drilling oil wells

Major reserves: Middle East, Russia, USA, Venezuela. In India: Mumbai High, Assam, Gujarat, Rajasthan.

Refining (Fractional Distillation)

Crude petroleum (crude oil) is a mixture of hundreds of compounds. Refining separates these into useful fractions based on their different boiling points.

  • Crude oil is heated in a furnace and fed into a tall fractional distillation column
  • Different fractions condense at different heights (temperatures)
  • Lighter fractions (lower boiling point) rise higher; heavier fractions condense lower
Crude Oil + Heat → Fractional Distillation → Multiple Products

Products from Petroleum

Fractional distillation of crude petroleum yields many valuable products at different temperature ranges:

  • LPG (Liquefied Petroleum Gas): Propane + butane gases; boiling point below 0°C. Used as cooking gas (domestic fuel). Burns cleanly and efficiently.
  • Petrol (Gasoline): Boiling range 40–75°C. Fuel for cars and motorcycles. Also used as dry cleaning solvent.
  • Naphtha: Boiling range 75–120°C. Feedstock for making chemicals, rubber, petrol.
  • Kerosene: Boiling range 150–250°C. Used in jet aircraft fuel (aviation turbine fuel/ATF), stoves, and lamps.
  • Diesel: Boiling range 250–350°C. Fuel for trucks, buses, ships, and generators.
  • Lubricating oil: Boiling range 350–400°C. Reduces friction between moving machine parts.
  • Paraffin wax: Used for making candles, wax paper, Vaseline, polishes.
  • Bitumen (Asphalt): Heaviest fraction; boiling point above 400°C. Used for road surfacing and waterproofing roofs.

Petroleum is called "liquid gold" because it produces so many essential products. It is also the raw material for making plastics, synthetic fibres, medicines, and thousands of other products (petrochemicals).

Conservation of Fossil Fuels

Since fossil fuels are non-renewable, conserving them is critically important for the future.

Why Conservation Matters

  • Fossil fuels will run out — oil in ~50 years, coal in ~200 years at current rates
  • Burning fossil fuels releases CO2, causing global warming and climate change
  • SO2 and NO2 from burning cause acid rain and respiratory diseases
  • Oil spills from tankers devastate marine ecosystems
  • Mining coal causes land degradation and habitat loss

How to Conserve

  • Use public transport instead of personal vehicles
  • Switch off engines at red lights and when not needed
  • Use energy-efficient appliances (LEDs, 5-star ratings)
  • Shift to renewable energy: Solar, wind, hydroelectric, geothermal
  • Carpooling and cycling for short distances
  • Insulate buildings to reduce heating/cooling needs
  • Use CNG (Compressed Natural Gas) for vehicles — burns cleaner than petrol/diesel

Alternative energy sources like solar energy, wind energy, tidal energy, and nuclear energy can help reduce our dependence on fossil fuels.

Chapter Summary

Key Concepts

  • Fossil fuels formed from dead organisms over millions of years — non-renewable
  • Coal types (peat→lignite→bituminous→anthracite) increase in carbon content
  • Coal products: coke, coal tar, coal gas (from destructive distillation)
  • Petroleum refined by fractional distillation into LPG, petrol, kerosene, diesel, bitumen
  • Burning fossil fuels causes pollution and climate change
  • Renewable energy and conservation are essential for the future

Key Terms

  • Fossil fuel: Fuel formed from ancient organisms
  • Carbonisation: Process of coal formation
  • Coke: Nearly pure carbon from coal
  • Coal tar: Complex liquid mixture from coal distillation
  • Crude oil: Unrefined petroleum as extracted from earth
  • Fractional distillation: Separating crude oil into products
  • Bitumen: Heaviest petroleum fraction; used for roads
  • Petrochemical: Chemical derived from petroleum

Every litre of petrol we save today helps preserve resources for future generations and reduces harmful emissions in our atmosphere.
