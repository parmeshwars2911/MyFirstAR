Crop Production and Management

Chapter 1 · Grade 8 Science

Food is essential for all living organisms. Plants make food through photosynthesis, and crops are cultivated plants grown on a large scale to meet our food needs.

Topics covered: Types of crops · Agricultural practices · Irrigation · Crop protection · Storage

Types of Crops

Crops are classified based on the season in which they are grown.

Kharif Crops

Sown in summer (June–July), harvested in autumn (September–October)

  • Paddy (rice)
  • Maize (corn)
  • Soybean
  • Groundnut
  • Cotton

Require plenty of water and warm weather

Rabi Crops

Sown in winter (October–November), harvested in spring (March–April)

  • Wheat
  • Gram (chickpea)
  • Mustard
  • Pea
  • Linseed

Require cool climate and less water

India also grows Zaid crops in short summer periods (between Rabi and Kharif) — e.g., watermelon, cucumber.

Agricultural Practices

Growing crops involves several systematic steps called agricultural practices.

  • Soil Preparation: Loosening and turning the soil using a plough (tilling/tilling). Loosened soil allows roots to penetrate deeper and absorbs water better.
  • Sowing: Placing seeds in the soil at proper depth and spacing. Traditional tools: seed drill (ensures uniform depth and spacing). Seeds must be healthy and free from disease.
  • Adding Manure and Fertilizers:
    • Manure – natural organic matter (compost, vermicompost, farmyard manure). Improves soil texture.
    • Fertilizers – chemical substances (urea, NPK). Provide specific nutrients. Overuse harms environment.
  • Irrigation: Supply of water to crops at regular intervals.
  • Weeding: Removal of unwanted plants (weeds) competing with crops.
  • Harvesting: Cutting and collecting mature crops. Done manually or by machines (combine harvester).
  • Storage: Proper storage prevents spoilage and waste.

The science of cultivating crops is called Agriculture. India's population depends heavily on agriculture for food and livelihood.

Irrigation Methods

Irrigation is the artificial supply of water to crops. Different methods suit different terrains and crop types.

Traditional Methods

  • Canal system: Water from rivers carried through canals to fields. Suitable for flat terrain.
  • Well: Water drawn from underground wells using pulleys or hand pumps.
  • Tube-well: Electric pumps draw groundwater through deep bore pipes. Widely used in India.
  • Moat (pulley system): Bucket on a rope, pulled by bullocks.
  • Chain pump: Series of discs on a chain lift water continuously.

Modern Methods

  • Sprinkler system: Water distributed through pipes with rotating sprinklers — mimics rain. Good for uneven land and sandy soil.
  • Drip irrigation: Water delivered drop by drop directly to the roots through narrow tubes. Most water-efficient method. Best for horticulture crops and water-scarce regions.

Drip irrigation saves up to 50% more water than flood irrigation.

Crop Protection

Crops are threatened by weeds, pests, and diseases. Effective protection is critical for good yield.

Types of Threats

  • Weeds: Unwanted plants growing along with crops (e.g., Amaranthus, wild oat). They compete for nutrients, water, and sunlight.
  • Insects and Pests: Attack roots, stems, leaves, or fruits (e.g., locusts, aphids, stem borers).
  • Diseases: Caused by fungi, bacteria, or viruses (e.g., rust of wheat, blast of rice).

Control Methods

  • Weedicides: Chemicals sprayed to kill weeds (e.g., 2,4-D). Must be used carefully to avoid harming crops.
  • Pesticides: Kill insect pests (e.g., malathion, BHC).
  • Fungicides: Kill fungal pathogens causing crop diseases.
  • Biological control: Using natural predators to control pests (eco-friendly).
  • Crop rotation: Growing different crops in succession reduces pest build-up.

Manual weeding by uprooting or tilling is the safest method but is labour-intensive. Weeding is best done before weeds flower and produce seeds.

Storage of Food Grains

After harvesting, proper storage prevents loss of food grains due to biological and environmental factors.

Causes of Spoilage

  • Moisture: Promotes growth of fungi and bacteria, causing rotting and germination.
  • Pests: Rats, insects, mites eat and contaminate stored grains.
  • Microorganisms: Fungi produce toxins (aflatoxins) that make food unsafe.
  • Temperature fluctuations affect grain quality.

Storage Methods and Structures

  • Jute bags / gunny bags: Traditional, used at farm level.
  • Metallic bins: Airtight containers protect against insects and moisture.
  • Silos: Large tall cylindrical structures for large-scale grain storage. Grains remain dry and pest-free.
  • Granaries (godowns): Large warehouses maintained by FCI (Food Corporation of India).
  • Cold storage: For fruits, vegetables, and perishables.

Before storage, grains are dried in the sun to reduce moisture content. Fumigation (using chemicals like aluminium phosphide) is used to kill insects in stored grain.

Chapter Summary

Key Concepts

  • Kharif crops grown in rainy season; Rabi crops in winter
  • Agricultural practices: preparation → sowing → irrigation → protection → harvest → storage
  • Manure is organic; fertilizers are chemical
  • Drip irrigation is most efficient for water conservation
  • Weedicides, pesticides, and fungicides protect crops
  • Silos and granaries store food grains safely

Key Terms

  • Kharif: Summer season crops
  • Rabi: Winter season crops
  • Tilling: Loosening soil before sowing
  • Irrigation: Artificial water supply to crops
  • Weedicide: Chemical that kills weeds
  • Silo: Large grain storage structure
  • Manure: Natural organic fertilizer
  • Seed drill: Tool for sowing seeds uniformly

Agriculture is the backbone of India's economy. Sustainable farming practices ensure food security for future generations.
