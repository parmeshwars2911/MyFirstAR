Microorganisms: Friend and Foe

Chapter 2 · Grade 8 Science

Microorganisms are living beings too small to be seen by the naked eye. They are found everywhere — in air, water, soil, and inside other organisms. Some help us; others can harm us.

Topics covered: Types of microorganisms · Useful roles · Harmful effects · Food preservation · Nitrogen cycle

Types of Microorganisms

Microorganisms (or microbes) are classified into the following major groups:

  • Bacteria: Single-celled prokaryotes. Found in soil, water, air, and in living organisms. Can be spherical (cocci), rod-shaped (bacilli), or spiral (spirilla). Example: Lactobacillus (makes curd), Rhizobium (fixes nitrogen).
  • Fungi: Multi- or unicellular eukaryotes with a cell wall. Obtain nutrition by decomposing organic matter. Examples: Aspergillus, Penicillium, bread mould (Rhizopus), yeast (Saccharomyces).
  • Protozoa: Unicellular organisms, mostly parasitic or free-living in water. Examples: Amoeba, Paramecium, Plasmodium (causes malaria).
  • Algae: Mostly aquatic, photosynthetic microorganisms. Unicellular or filamentous. Examples: Chlamydomonas, Spirogyra, Nostoc.
  • Viruses: Not truly alive — require a host cell to reproduce. Much smaller than bacteria. Cause diseases like flu, COVID-19, polio, rabies, dengue.

Viruses are not classified as living organisms by many scientists because they can only replicate inside a host cell. A microscope is required to observe all microorganisms.

Useful Microorganisms

Many microorganisms play vital positive roles in our lives and in nature.

In Food and Industry

  • Curd making: Lactobacillus bacteria ferment milk, converting lactose into lactic acid, which curdles milk into curd.
  • Bread and cake: Yeast (Saccharomyces cerevisiae) produces CO2 during fermentation, causing dough to rise.
  • Cheese and idli/dosa: Fermented using bacteria and fungi.
  • Alcohol production: Yeast ferments sugars to produce ethanol.
  • Vinegar: Acetic acid bacteria convert alcohol to acetic acid.

In Medicine and Agriculture

  • Antibiotics: Penicillium notatum produces penicillin (discovered by Alexander Fleming, 1928). Other antibiotics: streptomycin, erythromycin.
  • Vaccines: Weakened/dead microbes used to create immunity (e.g., polio vaccine, BCG for TB).
  • Nitrogen fixation: Rhizobium in root nodules of legumes converts atmospheric N2 to ammonia (NH3) usable by plants.
  • Decomposers: Break down dead organic matter, recycling nutrients back to soil.
  • Biofertilizers: Azotobacter, Nostoc (blue-green algae) enrich soil fertility.

Harmful Microorganisms

Some microorganisms cause diseases in humans, animals, and plants, or spoil food.

Diseases in Humans

  • Bacteria: Typhoid (Salmonella typhi), Cholera (Vibrio cholerae), Tuberculosis (Mycobacterium tuberculosis)
  • Viruses: Common cold, Influenza, Polio, Hepatitis, COVID-19, Dengue, Rabies
  • Fungi: Ringworm, Athlete's foot
  • Protozoa: Malaria (Plasmodium via Anopheles mosquito), Amoebic dysentery (Entamoeba histolytica)

Diseases in Animals and Plants

  • In animals: Anthrax (cattle), Foot-and-mouth disease, Bird flu
  • In plants: Citrus canker (bacteria), Rust of wheat (fungi), Yellow vein mosaic of bhindi (virus)
  • Food spoilage: Bacteria and fungi decompose stored food, making it unfit to eat

Communicable diseases spread through air, water, food, direct contact, or vectors like mosquitoes and flies.

Food Preservation Methods

Food preservation prevents the growth of microorganisms that cause spoilage. Different methods work for different types of food.

  • Salt: High concentration draws out water from microbes (osmosis), inhibiting their growth. Used for pickles, fish, meat.
  • Sugar: High sugar concentration prevents microbial growth. Used for jams, jellies, squashes, murabba.
  • Oil and Vinegar: Create an acidic/anaerobic environment unfavorable for microbes. Used for pickles.
  • Heat treatment:
    • Pasteurisation: Milk heated to 70°C for 15–30 seconds, then rapidly cooled. Kills harmful bacteria without destroying nutrients.
    • Boiling: Kills most pathogens in food and water.
  • Cold storage: Refrigeration slows microbial growth; freezing halts it almost completely.
  • Chemical preservatives: Sodium benzoate, sodium metabisulphite used in packaged foods and juices.
  • Dehydration (drying): Removing moisture prevents microbial growth. Used for pulses, chips, fish.

Pasteurisation was developed by Louis Pasteur and remains one of the most important food safety innovations in history.

The Nitrogen Cycle

Nitrogen is essential for making proteins and nucleic acids. Though 78% of air is nitrogen (N2), most organisms cannot use it directly — microorganisms play a key role in making it available.

N2 (atmosphere) → NH3 (ammonia) → Nitrites → Nitrates → Absorbed by plants → Proteins in plants/animals → Decomposers → Back to soil → Denitrification → N2 (atmosphere)
  • Nitrogen fixation: Bacteria like Rhizobium (in legume root nodules) and free-living bacteria (Azotobacter, Nostoc) convert N2 → NH3.
  • Nitrification: Soil bacteria convert NH3 → Nitrites → Nitrates (usable by plants).
  • Assimilation: Plants absorb nitrates through roots and make proteins. Animals get nitrogen by eating plants.
  • Ammonification: Decomposer bacteria and fungi break down dead organisms, releasing NH3 back into soil.
  • Denitrification: Some bacteria convert nitrates back to N2, returning it to the atmosphere.

Leguminous plants (peas, beans, groundnut) have symbiotic Rhizobium bacteria in their root nodules — this is why farmers rotate crops with legumes to restore soil nitrogen.

Chapter Summary

Key Concepts

  • 5 types of microorganisms: bacteria, fungi, protozoa, algae, viruses
  • Useful roles: food production, medicines (antibiotics), nitrogen fixation, decomposition
  • Harmful roles: diseases in humans, animals, and plants; food spoilage
  • Food is preserved by salt, sugar, oil, heat, cold, and chemicals
  • Nitrogen cycle depends on bacteria to make N2 available to plants
  • Vaccines and antibiotics protect us from microbial diseases

Key Terms

  • Pathogen: Disease-causing microorganism
  • Antibiotic: Medicine that kills bacteria
  • Vaccine: Weakened microbe that builds immunity
  • Fermentation: Breakdown of sugars by yeast/bacteria
  • Nitrogen fixation: Converting N2 to usable form
  • Pasteurisation: Heat treatment to kill pathogens in milk
  • Decomposer: Organism that breaks down dead matter

Microorganisms are invisible yet incredibly powerful — they sustain ecosystems, feed us through fermentation, heal us with medicines, and can also make us sick.
