Chapter 9

Reproduction in Animals

Exploring how animals produce offspring — from simple cell division to complex live birth

Grade 8 Science

Types of Reproduction

Reproduction is the biological process by which living organisms produce new individuals of the same kind. It ensures the continuation of a species.

Sexual Reproduction

  • Involves two parents (male and female)
  • Requires fusion of male and female gametes (fertilization)
  • Offspring are genetically different from parents
  • Examples: humans, birds, frogs, dogs

Asexual Reproduction

  • Involves only one parent
  • No gametes required
  • Offspring are genetically identical to parent
  • Budding in Hydra: A bud grows out of the parent's body, develops into a new individual, and eventually separates
  • Binary fission in Amoeba: The nucleus divides first, then the cytoplasm splits into two equal halves, each becoming a new Amoeba
Key Point: Asexual reproduction produces clones (identical copies), while sexual reproduction leads to genetic variation which is important for evolution and adaptation.

Sexual Reproduction in Animals

Sexual reproduction involves the production of special cells called gametes and their fusion in a process called fertilization.

Gametes: Male gametes are called sperms (small and motile). Female gametes are called eggs/ova (larger and non-motile). The fusion of sperm and egg produces a zygote.

Internal Fertilization

  • Fertilization occurs inside the female's body
  • More efficient — fewer gametes needed
  • Higher chance of offspring survival
  • Examples: humans, dogs, cats, birds, reptiles

External Fertilization

  • Fertilization occurs outside the female's body
  • Occurs in water or moist environments
  • Large number of eggs and sperms released to increase chances
  • Examples: frogs, fish, starfish, most aquatic animals

The zygote undergoes repeated cell division (mitosis) to develop into an embryo, which eventually becomes a fully formed organism.

Development: Viviparous vs Oviparous

After fertilization, animals develop and give birth to offspring in different ways depending on the species.

Viviparous Animals

Give birth to young ones that are fully or partially developed.
  • The embryo develops inside the mother's uterus
  • Nourished through the placenta
  • Young ones resemble the parent
  • Examples: Humans, dogs, cats, elephants, dolphins, whales, bats
  • Gestation period: time from fertilization to birth (humans: ~9 months)

Oviparous Animals

Lay eggs; the embryo develops inside the egg outside the mother's body.
  • Eggs may be incubated by parents (birds) or left in water (fish, frogs)
  • Eggs provide all nutrition needed for development
  • Large numbers of eggs laid (less parental care in most cases)
  • Examples: Birds, frogs, fish, reptiles, insects, most amphibians
  • Incubation period: time for egg to hatch

Metamorphosis

Metamorphosis is the process by which some animals undergo dramatic changes in body form and structure as they develop from juvenile to adult stage.

Complete Metamorphosis

Four distinct stages:

  • Egg → laid by adult female
  • Larva (caterpillar) → hatches from egg, feeds and grows
  • Pupa (chrysalis/cocoon) → resting stage, tissues reorganize completely
  • Adult (butterfly/moth) → fully formed, can reproduce

Examples: Butterfly, moth, beetle, housefly, bee, mosquito

Incomplete Metamorphosis

Three stages — no pupa stage:

  • Egg → laid by adult female
  • Nymph → looks like a miniature adult, moults several times
  • Adult → fully developed wings and reproductive organs

Examples: Grasshopper, cockroach, dragonfly, termite, cricket

Nymphs gradually develop wings and other adult features through each moult.

Cloning and Test-Tube Babies

Cloning

Cloning is the process of producing a genetically identical copy of an organism using its DNA (asexual reproduction by artificial means).
  • Dolly the Sheep (1996) — the first mammal to be cloned from an adult somatic cell
  • Created by scientists at the Roslin Institute in Scotland
  • Technique used: Somatic Cell Nuclear Transfer (SCNT) — nucleus from a body cell of one sheep was placed into an enucleated egg cell
  • Dolly was genetically identical to the sheep that donated the nucleus
  • Dolly lived for 6 years and gave birth to healthy lambs

Test-Tube Babies (In Vitro Fertilization — IVF)

IVF is a technique where fertilization takes place outside the body, in a laboratory dish ("in vitro" means "in glass").
  • Eggs are collected from the mother and fertilized with sperm in a lab
  • The resulting embryo is implanted into the mother's uterus
  • Helps couples who cannot conceive naturally
  • First test-tube baby: Louise Brown, born in England in 1978

Chapter 9 Summary: Reproduction in Animals

  • Asexual reproduction: One parent; genetically identical offspring — Budding (Hydra), Binary fission (Amoeba)
  • Sexual reproduction: Two parents; fusion of gametes (sperm + egg = zygote); genetic variation in offspring
  • Fertilization: Internal (inside body: humans, birds) or External (outside body: frogs, fish)
  • Viviparous: Give birth to young ones (humans, dogs, cats); embryo develops in uterus
  • Oviparous: Lay eggs (birds, frogs, fish); embryo develops inside egg
  • Complete metamorphosis: Egg → Larva → Pupa → Adult (butterfly, moth)
  • Incomplete metamorphosis: Egg → Nymph → Adult (grasshopper, cockroach)
  • Cloning: Producing genetically identical copies; Dolly the sheep (1996) was the first cloned mammal
  • IVF (Test-tube baby): Fertilization outside the body in a lab; embryo implanted into uterus
