Chapter 10

Reaching the Age of Adolescence

Understanding the physical, emotional, and hormonal changes that shape your teenage years

Grade 8 Science

What is Adolescence?

Adolescence is the period of life between childhood and adulthood, typically from age 11 to 18 years. People in this age group are called teenagers or adolescents.
  • The word "adolescence" comes from the Latin word adolescere meaning "to grow up"
  • It is marked by the onset of puberty — the time when the body begins to develop and change as it prepares for adulthood
  • Puberty begins earlier in girls (around age 10–12) than in boys (around age 12–14)
  • The changes during adolescence are driven by hormones released by endocrine glands

Why is Adolescence Important?

  • The body becomes capable of reproduction
  • Significant emotional and psychological development occurs
  • Identity, values, and social relationships are shaped during this period

Changes at Puberty

Puberty brings about a wide range of physical changes that are common to both boys and girls, as well as some that are specific to each.

Common Changes in Both Boys and Girls

  • Increase in height: The most rapid growth in height occurs during puberty — this is called the pubertal growth spurt. Bones grow longer under the influence of growth hormone.
  • Body hair: Hair begins to grow in the armpits and the pubic region
  • Sweating: Sweat glands become more active, sometimes causing body odour
  • Pimples and acne: Oil-secreting sebaceous glands in the skin become overactive; pores can get blocked, causing pimples
  • Voice change: The voice box (larynx) grows larger; in boys this is more prominent, causing a deeper, cracking voice
The growth of the larynx in boys sometimes shows as a Adam's apple — a visible lump at the front of the throat.

Secondary Sexual Characters

Secondary sexual characters are features that distinguish males from females but are not directly part of the reproductive system. They appear during puberty under the influence of sex hormones.

In Males (Boys)

  • Growth of facial hair (beard and moustache)
  • Deeper voice (due to growth of larynx)
  • Broader shoulders and muscular build
  • Growth of hair on chest, armpits, and pubic region
  • Testes begin producing sperms
  • Increased muscle mass

In Females (Girls)

  • Development of breasts
  • Widening of the hips (pelvis broadens)
  • Growth of pubic and underarm hair
  • Ovaries begin releasing eggs (ovulation)
  • Menstruation begins — monthly discharge of blood and mucous membrane lining from the uterus (menstrual cycle: ~28 days)
  • Higher pitched voice

Menstruation stops during pregnancy and ceases permanently at menopause (around age 45–50).

Hormones and Endocrine Glands

Hormones are chemical messengers secreted by endocrine (ductless) glands directly into the bloodstream. They regulate growth, development, and many body functions.

Key Sex Hormones

  • Testosterone: Male sex hormone; produced by testes; responsible for secondary sexual characters in boys and sperm production
  • Estrogen: Female sex hormone; produced by ovaries; responsible for secondary sexual characters in girls and regulation of menstrual cycle
  • Growth Hormone (GH): Produced by the pituitary gland; controls overall body growth

Important Endocrine Glands

  • Pituitary gland (master gland): Controls all other endocrine glands; secretes growth hormone
  • Thyroid gland: Regulates metabolism; secretes thyroxine
  • Adrenal glands: Secrete adrenaline ("fight or flight" hormone)
  • Pancreas: Secretes insulin (regulates blood sugar)
  • Testes: Secrete testosterone (in males)
  • Ovaries: Secrete estrogen and progesterone (in females)

Reproductive Health and Healthy Habits

Taking care of your physical and emotional health during adolescence is extremely important for overall well-being and healthy development.

Healthy Practices

  • Balanced diet: Eat plenty of fruits, vegetables, proteins, and whole grains; iron is especially important for girls to compensate for blood loss during menstruation
  • Personal hygiene: Regular bathing, dental care, and keeping hair and nails clean; especially important as sweat glands are more active
  • Physical activity: Regular exercise strengthens bones, muscles, and supports mental health
  • Adequate sleep: 8–9 hours of sleep is essential for growth and brain development
  • Regular medical check-ups: Monitor growth and address any health concerns

Things to Avoid

  • Drugs and alcohol: Severely harmful to the developing brain and body; can cause addiction and long-term damage
  • Smoking/tobacco: Causes lung disease, cancer, and stunts growth
  • Junk food and excess sugar: Leads to obesity, diabetes, and nutritional deficiencies
  • Stress and peer pressure: Learn to say no; talk to trusted adults about problems
Adolescence is a time of emotional ups and downs. It is completely normal to feel confused, self-conscious, or moody. Talk to parents, counselors, or trusted friends for support.

Chapter 10 Summary: Reaching the Age of Adolescence

  • Adolescence: Age 11–18 years; teenagers; begins with puberty
  • Puberty changes (both): Height increase (growth spurt), body hair, sweating, acne/pimples, voice change
  • Secondary sexual characters — Males: Facial hair, deeper voice, broad shoulders, sperm production begins
  • Secondary sexual characters — Females: Breast development, wider hips, menstruation begins (~28-day cycle)
  • Key hormones: Testosterone (male, from testes), Estrogen (female, from ovaries), Growth Hormone (from pituitary)
  • Endocrine glands: Pituitary (master gland), thyroid, adrenal, pancreas, testes, ovaries — all secrete hormones directly into blood
  • Healthy habits: Balanced diet (iron-rich for girls), personal hygiene, regular exercise, adequate sleep, medical check-ups
  • Avoid: Drugs, alcohol, tobacco, junk food, unhealthy peer pressure
  • Emotional changes are normal; seek support from trusted adults when needed
