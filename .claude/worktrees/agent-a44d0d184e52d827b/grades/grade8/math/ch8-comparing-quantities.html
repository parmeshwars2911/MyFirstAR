Chapter 8 · Grade 8 Mathematics

Ratios and Percentages

Ratios

A ratio compares two quantities of the same kind. The ratio of a to b is written as a : b or a/b. Ratios are simplified like fractions.

Example: If a class has 30 boys and 20 girls, the ratio of boys to girls = 30 : 20 = 3 : 2.

Percentages

A percentage is a ratio with denominator 100. It means "out of 100" and is written with the symbol %.

Percentage = (Part / Whole) × 100

Fraction to Percentage

  • 3/4 = (3/4) × 100 = 75%
  • 1/5 = (1/5) × 100 = 20%
  • 7/20 = (7/20) × 100 = 35%
  • 3/8 = (3/8) × 100 = 37.5%

Percentage to Fraction

  • 40% = 40/100 = 2/5
  • 25% = 25/100 = 1/4
  • 12.5% = 12.5/100 = 1/8
  • 66.67% = 2/3 (approximately)

Percentage Increase and Decrease

Percentage Increase = (Increase / Original Value) × 100
Percentage Decrease = (Decrease / Original Value) × 100

Example 1: A price increases from Rs. 400 to Rs. 500. Increase = 100. % Increase = (100/400) × 100 = 25%.

Example 2: A population fell from 8000 to 7200. Decrease = 800. % Decrease = (800/8000) × 100 = 10%.

Note: Percentage change is always calculated with respect to the original (starting) value, not the new value.

Profit and Loss

Key Terms

  • Cost Price (CP): The price at which an article is purchased (bought).
  • Selling Price (SP): The price at which an article is sold.
  • Marked Price (MP): The price marked on an article (also called list price or tag price).
  • Profit: When SP > CP, the seller makes a profit.
  • Loss: When CP > SP, the seller incurs a loss.
  • Discount: A reduction given on the marked price. Discount = MP – SP.

Profit Formulas

Profit = SP – CP   (when SP > CP)
Profit% = (Profit / CP) × 100
SP = CP × (1 + Profit%/100)

If Profit% is known: SP = [(100 + Profit%) / 100] × CP

Loss Formulas

Loss = CP – SP   (when CP > SP)
Loss% = (Loss / CP) × 100
SP = CP × (1 – Loss%/100)

If Loss% is known: SP = [(100 – Loss%) / 100] × CP

Discount

Discount = MP – SP     Discount% = (Discount / MP) × 100
SP = MP – Discount = MP × (1 – Discount%/100)

Worked Example

A shopkeeper buys a fan for Rs. 1200 and marks it at Rs. 1600. He sells it at a 15% discount. Find SP, and determine whether he makes a profit or loss and by what percentage.

Discount = 15% of 1600 = 0.15 × 1600 = Rs. 240.   SP = 1600 – 240 = Rs. 1360.   Since SP (1360) > CP (1200), it is a Profit.   Profit = 1360 – 1200 = Rs. 160.   Profit% = (160/1200) × 100 = 13.33%.

Simple Interest (SI)

When money is borrowed or invested, an extra amount is charged or earned, called interest. In simple interest, the interest is calculated on the original principal every year (it does not change year-on-year).

Key Terms

  • Principal (P): The original sum of money borrowed or invested.
  • Rate of Interest (R): The percentage of principal charged as interest per year (per annum).
  • Time (T): The duration for which the money is borrowed or invested (in years).
  • Simple Interest (SI): Interest computed on the original principal for the entire period.
  • Amount (A): The total money returned at the end = Principal + Interest.
SI = (P × R × T) / 100
Amount (A) = P + SI = P + (P × R × T)/100 = P × [1 + (R × T)/100]

Worked Example

Problem: Ravi borrows Rs. 5000 at a rate of 8% per annum simple interest for 3 years. Find the interest and total amount to be repaid.

P = 5000, R = 8%, T = 3 years

SI = (5000 × 8 × 3) / 100 = 120000 / 100 = Rs. 1200
Amount = P + SI = 5000 + 1200 = Rs. 6200

Finding P, R, or T from SI

  • P = (SI × 100) / (R × T)
  • R = (SI × 100) / (P × T)
  • T = (SI × 100) / (P × R)

Example: SI = Rs. 600, P = Rs. 4000, T = 2 years. Find R. R = (600 × 100)/(4000 × 2) = 60000/8000 = 7.5% per annum.

Compound Interest (CI)

In compound interest, the interest for each period is added to the principal before computing interest for the next period. Interest is earned "on interest". This makes money grow faster than simple interest.

Amount (A) = P × (1 + R/100)^n
Compound Interest (CI) = A – P = P × [(1 + R/100)^n – 1]

Where: P = Principal, R = Rate % per annum, n = number of years (compounding periods).

Worked Example

Problem: Find the CI on Rs. 5000 at 8% per annum for 3 years, compounded annually. Compare with SI for the same values.

Year 1: Interest = 8% of 5000 = Rs. 400. Amount at end of Year 1 = Rs. 5400.

Year 2: Interest = 8% of 5400 = Rs. 432. Amount at end of Year 2 = Rs. 5832.

Year 3: Interest = 8% of 5832 = Rs. 466.56. Amount at end of Year 3 = Rs. 6298.56.

Using formula: A = 5000 × (1 + 8/100)³ = 5000 × (1.08)³ = 5000 × 1.259712 = Rs. 6298.56
CI = 6298.56 – 5000 = Rs. 1298.56

Comparison: CI vs SI

Same: P = 5000, R = 8%, T = 3 years

SI = (5000 × 8 × 3)/100 = Rs. 1200

CI = Rs. 1298.56

Difference = 1298.56 – 1200 = Rs. 98.56

CI > SI for the same P, R, T (after the first year)

Why CI > SI?

In SI, interest is always calculated on the original principal (5000) each year.

In CI, interest is calculated on an increasing principal each year. After Year 1, the principal becomes 5400 (not 5000), so Year 2 earns more interest.

The difference grows with time and rate.

Applications of Compound Interest

1. Population Growth

If a city's population grows at a fixed rate of R% per year, the population after n years is found using the compound interest formula:

P_n = P_0 × (1 + R/100)^n

Where P_0 = initial population, R = growth rate % per year, n = number of years, P_n = population after n years.

Example: A town's population is 50,000 in 2020. It grows at 4% per year. Find the population in 2023 (after 3 years).

P_3 = 50000 × (1 + 4/100)³ = 50000 × (1.04)³ = 50000 × 1.124864 = 56243.2 ≈ 56,243

The population grows by approximately 6243 people over 3 years.

2. Depreciation (Decrease in Value)

Physical assets like machines, vehicles, and electronics lose value over time. This reduction in value is called depreciation. If the rate of depreciation is R% per year, the value after n years is:

V_n = V_0 × (1 – R/100)^n

Where V_0 = initial value, R = depreciation rate % per year, n = number of years, V_n = value after n years.

Example: A car is purchased for Rs. 6,00,000. Its value depreciates at 10% per year. Find its value after 3 years.

V_3 = 600000 × (1 – 10/100)³ = 600000 × (0.90)³ = 600000 × 0.729 = Rs. 4,37,400

The car's value falls by Rs. 1,62,600 over 3 years due to depreciation.

Summary of Application Formulas

Situation Formula Key Difference
Population GrowthP_n = P_0 × (1 + R/100)^nValue increases (+ sign)
DepreciationV_n = V_0 × (1 – R/100)^nValue decreases (– sign)
Compound InterestA = P × (1 + R/100)^nMoney grows (+ sign)

Percentage Formulas

Percentage = (Part / Whole) × 100
% Increase = (Increase / Original) × 100
% Decrease = (Decrease / Original) × 100

Profit, Loss and Discount

Profit = SP – CP   |   Loss = CP – SP
Profit% = (Profit/CP) × 100
Loss% = (Loss/CP) × 100
Discount = MP – SP
Discount% = (Discount/MP) × 100
SP = MP × (1 – Discount%/100)

Simple Interest

SI = (P × R × T) / 100
A = P + SI

Compound Interest

A = P × (1 + R/100)^n
CI = A – P

Applications

Growth: P_n = P_0 × (1 + R/100)^n
Depreciation: V_n = V_0 × (1 – R/100)^n

Key Relationships

  • CI > SI for same P, R, T (after the first year)
  • In Year 1, CI and SI are equal
  • Profit% and Loss% are always based on CP
  • Discount% is always based on MP

Quick Check: For profit/loss — always use CP as base. For discount — always use MP as base. For CI — remember the (1 + R/100)^n formula. Growth uses +R; depreciation uses –R in the bracket.
